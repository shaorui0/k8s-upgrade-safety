# 0004-contracts-and-reconcile notes

- The change that mattered was moving the decisions out of the engine and into data. Before, the engine poked at the cluster dict ("if readyz is false, fail"). Now a step is a record with prechecks and postchecks, probes answer questions about a snapshot, and the engine only interprets. Any new gate is a new assertion, not new engine code.
- The contracts earn their keep the first time you try to write them down. Writing the snapshot schema forced the question "what does a probe need to see?", and the answer (eviction time, PDB numbers, LB state, backups) is exactly what the control plane must be able to observe. Anything the engine decided from hidden state became a snapshot field.
- Control plane versus execution plane is a testable seam, not a diagram. A test wraps the executor, runs a whole upgrade, and asserts every mutation went through `execute()`. Another asserts that an irreversible step refused for lack of a backup never reached the executor at all.
- Reversibility is a declared property, so "snapshot first" stopped being a special case. The control-plane component steps are irreversible and name `etcd-snapshot-fresh`; the snapshot step is merely what satisfies it. A stale snapshot (cluster moved 5000 raft entries on) fails the same rule.
- `already_done` is the whole idempotency story. A re-run asks "is the difference gone?" and skips if so. It is recorded as `converged_skipped`. The first version also wrote the "not done yet" answer into evidence as a failed assertion, which made every healthy first run look like it had failures. A negative `already_done` is not evidence of anything; only a positive one is recorded.
- Manual gate semantics are the old framework's: a failed postcheck under manual is `halted_by_operator`, not `stopped_postcheck`. The distinction (who stopped it) is going to matter when assisted and auto arrive.
- Plan is a diff with a verdict, and it computes reversibility per change. One irreversible change makes the whole diff irreversible; that is what the gate should be hardest about. Drift (ASG, launch template and nodes disagreeing) shows up as `alignment`, a difference to show before running, not a surprise during.
- Blast radius has two axes and conflating them is easy. Layer says what first (master, worker, components). Tier says how many at once inside a layer (canary of one, then at most 20%). The canary is a step of its own, which is why it can be tested to precede every batch.
- Writing the tests against the new artifacts found nothing wrong in the engine and one thing wrong in the tests: a failure assertion that passed because of a stale reading of the evidence. Mutation check went 13 to 19 gates; all killed.
